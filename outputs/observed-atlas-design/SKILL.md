# Observed Atlas System

Develop an artistic proposition through a subject, its possible forms, and its relationships. The subject may be a recognizable being or object, a collective, or several different forms that express one proposition. A requested subject establishes the work's focus; it does not prescribe the number of objects, a solitary hero, or the whole content of the image.

When the skill first applies, state why in one sentence and proceed. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the result. The user's explicit subject, style, mood, and medium override defaults.

## Routing

Apply OAS when the user names it, requests an operation such as tracing or sampling, or gives an open-ended theme whose states or relations can organize an artistic image. Infer useful relations without requiring method vocabulary.

Do not apply it to a plain style request, a fully specified documentary treatment, a routine UI or copy edit, a pure scene illustration, or work explicitly assigned to another visual language.

## Proposition before technique

Start with an internal interpretive sentence: what situation, association, tension, change, or way of seeing should the work make perceptible through this subject? Determine the intended first feeling as well as the first recognizable content. Poetic ambiguity may remain; explanatory copy must not supply all the meaning.

Use this order to develop the work:

> **proposition → subject forms, states, and world relations → compositional relationships → representation and material → perceptible consequence**

Consider relations within a subject, between different forms or actors, with a world or cultural context, across time and scale, or through absence and the picture's boundaries. Choose what strengthens this proposition. A single form can carry a powerful relation through gesture, crop, scale, interval, or negative space; additional objects and multiple states are not compulsory.

Select the subject for emotional, cultural, and thematic fit before considering how easy it is to dissect or annotate. Do not default to insects, specimens, organs, machinery, or exposed anatomy for analytical convenience. A user-named or conceptually indispensable subject remains valid.

The operational chain **subject → observation operation → structural response → evidence trail** remains useful when observation clarifies the chosen proposition. It is a means of execution, not a requirement to turn every subject into a specimen. Etching, fading, fragmentation, and material conversion do not complete a concept by themselves.

Establish the roles the composition needs:

- **Semantic anchors:** sufficient recognizable content to orient the viewer; an anchor may be continuous, fragmented, or distributed.
- **Relational field or carrier:** concrete content, abstract representation, or their interaction that organizes the proposition. Derive an abstract carrier from the subject's behavior when useful; do not require abstraction in every work.
- **Visible consequences and connections:** differences, contact, interruption, traces, or intervals that make the selected relation perceptible. Supporting metaphors retain a readable origin, target, or effect unless deliberate ambiguity belongs to the brief.
- **Situated world:** consider relevant historical, cultural, environmental, social, architectural, technical, or material content while framing the work. Retain it when it changes meaning or tension. Replace a reference's contextual role with a theme-specific equivalent rather than stripping away its world or copying its scenery.
- **Compositional scaffold:** normally establish a related family of axes, arcs, fields, blocks, frames, crops, or routes for OAS posters and key visuals. Omit it when it would weaken the proposition or the user selects another system, not simply to make the page quieter.

Concrete setting nouns commit the image to a world. Choose them deliberately within the brief's mood and cultural distance. A situated world can exist as drawn, printed, mapped, collaged, or interrupted fragments on a shallow plane; planarity does not require removing places, actors, or events.

## Dynamic subject analysis: change and unity

Before fixing one hero or effect, consider which dimensions can vary meaningfully: time, scale, material, structure, function, agency, perspective, visibility, preservation, or relationship. Select the forms needed by the proposition and determine what remains continuous. Do not invent stages or force a lifecycle when the subject does not call for one.

Distinguish two kinds of unity:

- **Identity continuity:** views or states of the same being or object retain defining anatomy, topology, load paths, structural axes, or recognizable source relationships.
- **Propositional continuity:** different objects, actors, or forms belong through a shared origin, action, dependence, memory, opposition, thematic relation, or compositional route. They need not share one anatomy or physically join into one body.

Organize the selected forms through progression, recurrence, branching, comparison, coexistence, overlap, rupture, or another subject-derived relation. Re-derive the topology for each work. A seed-to-tree sequence, a central void, a diagonal hero, and a triptych are possibilities, not reusable answers.

Every major state or fragment must contribute a distinct role or information about change: structure, material, time, preservation, function, agency, relationship, or a meaningful drawing regime. Copies differentiated only by color, texture, opacity, position, sharpness, crop, or damaged edges are insufficient. A missing region must expose another layer, state, or consequence. Conceptually remove decorative texture and geometry, then ask whether the differences still matter.

Information and integration are separate judgments. An anatomical or structural insert needs a credible cut, joint, axis, material passage, overlap, or continued drawing. Heterogeneous forms may connect through contact, alignment, shared space, scale relationships, or a meaningful route across an interval. Do not turn an informative section into a floating badge, or force different actors into an artificial physical seam.

Preserve essential anatomy, connections, scale, support, topology, and functional logic. Deliberate shifts of scale, impossible coexistence, and representational changes can serve a proposition; accidental dislocations, fused joints, unsupported assemblies, and invented convenient interiors do not. Derive the mechanism anew instead of importing bones, roots, wings, melting, or fragmentation as universal shorthand.

## Classical, philosophical, and quietly sacred register

Unless the user selects another direction, favor a classical sensibility informed by ancient Greek proportion, poise, and the relation of individual to whole, together with Central European traditions of humanist drawing, printmaking, and paper-based art. Let the register be reflective, dignified, and capable of quiet sacredness. Adapt its intensity to the subject and brief rather than making every work solemn or every figure idealized.

Express this sensibility through proportion, measured intervals, rhythm, credible living forms, restraint, and the tension between order and organic variation. Drawing, engraving, etching, chalk, underdrawing, correction, erasure, incomplete contours, paper collage, and material transfer may provide the craft language. Choose the relevant behaviors; do not blend every tradition or equalize every part's finish.

Philosophical depth can emerge through potential and manifestation, finite and infinite, individual and collective, becoming and loss, or another relation grounded in the subject. Sacred or ritual feeling may come through gesture, orientation, silence, scale, spacing, and attention. Columns, gods, crosses, halos, altars, ruins, gold, and religious emblems are subject matter to choose when meaningful, not a required symbol set. This default does not require Greek, Central European, or religious subject matter.

Derive tonal mood and chroma from the proposition. Paper tone, charcoal, warm gray-brown, subdued blue, earth color, or limited stronger accents can support the language without fixing a palette. Dark fields, intense contrast, or coarse surfaces carry emotional meaning; do not inherit them as neutral sophistication. Sacredness and philosophy do not automatically call for grimness, bodily uncanniness, or dramatic lighting.

## Depiction, detail, and emotional hierarchy

Name the subject's familiar default depiction and avoid letting it organize the entire image unless it actively carries the chosen relation. Surface texture, archival paper, translucent overlays, and signal-color edges alone do not create interpretive lift.

Translate the intended emotion into posture, proportion, gesture, edge, tonal modeling, and detail distribution before adding general mood adjectives. Concrete instructions for severe skin, hard joints, dense hatching, or enlarged bodily impressions can defeat a later request for tenderness. Keep prompt instructions internally consistent.

For human and animal content, distinguish structural credibility from exhaustive surface description. Preserve identity, age, anatomy, and necessary contact with selective marks. Skin, folds, veins, tendons, nails, muscle definition, and local volume become focal content only when the proposition or user requires them. Age can remain visible without rendering every crease sharply; preserve meaningful roughness when hardship, mortality, or a coarse portrait is explicitly intended.

Rank information inside the subject as well as across the page. Gesture, contact, and essential structure may receive decisive edges; supporting surfaces can use open contours, broken hatching, paper gaps, underdrawing, and local omission. High-resolution output allows selective depiction. Classical handmade credibility comes from authored choices and varying completion, not maximum engraving density.

Avoid involuntary clinical, grotesque, threatening, or tactile readings when the intended emotion is tender, dignified, contemplative, or quietly sacred. Examine the combined effect of viewpoint, scale, flexion, dark creases, edge sharpness, and repeated skin-like patterns. Unexpectedness may arise through relationships, time, material, or scale without intensifying bodily distress. Correct the drawing decisions rather than applying global blur or indiscriminate smoothing.

In multi-figure work, vary contour confidence, line pressure, hatching, transfer quality, crop, and completion while preserving credible anatomy and contact. Repeated faces, physiques, poses, garment folds, uniformly dramatic gestures, and equal edge finish create synthetic sameness. Meaningful variation does not justify one figure becoming excessively harsh merely to contrast with another.

## Medium, space, and composition

The primary visual layer of OAS posters and key visuals must be generated or assembled from raster, photographic, scanned, collage, or mixed-media material, then opened and accepted. Primitive SVG paths, flat vector blocks, and text must not replace that production. SVG, HTML, CSS, or another deterministic layer may implement precise typography, masks, geometry, and final compositing afterward. A user-requested pure-vector direction is a different permitted outcome.

Choose a representation regime from the proposition: drawing, print, engraving, scan, collage, flattened photography, material transfer, or a deliberate hybrid. Line drawing is one layer, not a reason to discard meaningful state differences, material counterpoints, or compositional structure. Photographic source material must not automatically become a seamless cinematic scene.

Unless realism, spatial installation, a cinematic scene, or another depth-led treatment is explicitly requested, all candidates share a planar, shallow field. Use frontal, side, near-orthographic, or compressed views with layered subjects and materials. Do not organize the work through a distant horizon, cinematic sky, depth of field, volumetric rays, realistic cast shadows, or sculptural CG space. Local volume must also remain subordinate: continuous gradients, wrapped highlights, occlusion, or dense tonal hatching must not make one limb, branch, member, or fragment a sculptural exception. Material richness and visible worlds can coexist with shallow space.

For poster production without a user reference, read [positive-anchors.md](references/positive-anchors.md), select one primary anchor by relational behavior, and pass its asset as an actual image-generation reference. A prose paraphrase is insufficient. Add a secondary anchor only for a distinct needed behavior. Adapt the reference's scale, density, finish, and material cues to the new subject and intended feeling; architectural or mechanical detail is not a default for skin. Do not ask for another upload when bundled anchors already provide the reference language.

Resolve large relationships before notation:

- Decide what registers first, including the intended emotion. For a collective, derive topology and shared space from contact, orientation, route, density, or another relation before completing individual figures. Rings, chains, bands, branches, weaves, clusters, convergence, and dispersal can all serve a group.
- Preserve meaning-bearing complexity while ranking anchors, states, carriers, geometry, context, surface detail, and color. Useful structural information and redundant optical texture are different. High contrast, edge frequency, repetition, and reach need not cover every part of the main subject.
- Vary density in connected regions. Quiet space may carry sparse low-contrast continuations of an established route for scale, direction, or tension. Do not leave accidentally disconnected fields, invent a second geometric system, or fill intervals with evenly scattered noise.
- When geometry is central, couple area-bearing fields, masks, crops, or blocks with sparse axes, arcs, or long routes. They share an organizing relationship and do visible work at more than one scale. Thin overlays on a complete scene, one inert block with a token arc, and unrelated dominant gestures do not retain a scaffold. Detail windows and indices are optional.
- Supporting metaphors remain readable through the anchor. Regulate their area, contrast, density, repetition, and proximity before removing a valid metaphor that overpowers the subject.
- During revision, distinguish new mark-making and lower salience from deletion of functional roles. Preserve or deliberately re-derive meaningful states, mixed material, context, area geometry, and long-range organization. Redundant surface detail may be omitted; do not collapse the accepted work into a uniform isolated illustration.
- Plan the role or absence of typography from the start. Implement exact copy separately and editably after accepting the visual layer. Do not generate final lettering or pseudo-text. Visible copy belongs to the subject rather than internal workflow or self-justification; factual-looking readings, IDs, coordinates, or dates need provenance or unmistakable illustrative labeling. Ordinary compositional indices may remain.

## Poster and key-visual workflow

1. **Frame.** Establish the proposition, intended first feeling, subject fit, possible states and world relations, what changes and what unifies it, default depiction to avoid, representation, tonal range, scaffold, and typography's role. For several candidates, define a shared visual-language envelope: emotional register, temporal and cultural distance, medium and craft, tonal behavior, and documentary-to-abstract distance.
2. **Explore.** For an open-ended poster, generate and open at least two actual text-free raster or mixed-media candidates with materially different propositions or relationship structures inside that envelope. Compare actors or forms, what happens between them, what changes, and how space carries the relation. Renaming concepts while retaining the same hero-to-print-to-fade mechanism, rotating a subject, or changing lighting, texture, or color is insufficient. Do not lock an open subject to the first convenient specimen; consider other anchors when they improve thematic and emotional fit. Described alternatives, code-drawn SVG plans, and unopened files are not candidates.
3. **Build.** Resolve anchors, selected states, their unity, world content, carrier when useful, visible consequences, geometric system, and detail hierarchy. Put the relation in the main visual rather than mainly in labels or analytical overlays. State each reference's role and prevent subject, motif, layout, and depiction-density copying.
4. **Typeset.** When text adds value, compose accurate editable copy and precise overlays in a deterministic layer using the already planned relationship to the visual.
5. **Review.** Compare the actual candidates against the same envelope and proposition. Open the final render at target size, thumbnail scale, and in grayscale. Check first emotion as well as first recognition; relational clarity and correct anatomy do not excuse an unintended clinical, threatening, or grotesque impression. Inspect sensitive bodily details at full size. Conceptually remove titles, texture, nonessential geometry, and atmosphere: meaning, state differences, and unity should remain. Verify shallow overall space and consistent local depth, fragment information and appropriate coupling, collective topology and authored variation, quiet fields, geometric mass and routes, and readable metaphor weight. For revisions, account for the previous functional roles. Reject failed candidates rather than selecting the least-bad one or hiding the failure with labels, paper texture, or blur.

Do not create audit artifacts merely to prove stages happened. Deliver the final output, editable source when relevant, and concise review conclusions.

## Acceptance

Accept only when the actual render supports the relevant claims:

- The artistic proposition and intended emotional register are perceptible through subject forms, relationships, or compositional tension without dependence on explanatory copy. Representation change is a means, not the sole artistic claim.
- Candidate differences exist in the actual relationship or state organization while the shared visual-language envelope holds. Coherent surface styling does not redeem a repeated mechanism or a switch into an unrequested genre.
- The recognizable content and any abstract carrier have grounded roles. The work surpasses a default depiction with decorative effects; a single subject may pass when its gesture, boundary, scale, interval, or other relationship actively carries meaning.
- Forms retain essential identity and structural credibility. Distinct states or heterogeneous forms contribute non-interchangeable meaning and belong through identity or a readable proposition; appropriate seams or relational connections integrate them.
- Unless overridden, classical craft, proportion, reflective attention, and a capacity for quiet sacredness appear in the image's organization and marks. A symbol checklist, old-paper filter, coarse skin, or grimness does not establish this register.
- Overall and local space obey the selected planar regime. Dense hatching is checked for excessive bodily or sculptural volume just as smooth modeling is.
- Hierarchy operates inside the subject and across the page. Gesture, relation, and necessary structure lead over irrelevant surface resolution; age and meaningful complexity survive selective depiction. Quiet intervals, palette, and metaphors support the intended reading.
- Geometry has a stable evidential or compositional job. When central, area and line families share a route or hierarchy and affect mass, crop, overlap, counterweight, or long-range connection.
- Group topology and representation variation precede separate complete vignettes or uniformly polished figures. Style revisions preserve functional richness while changing salience and mark-making.
- The chosen production medium, actual reference inputs, controlled optional typography, and provenance of factual-looking content meet their boundaries. The final composite was rendered and viewed; hashes, valid XML, and stated intentions do not prove visual success.

When a judgment fails, correct the earliest failed proposition, relationship, depiction, or composition decision. Additional texture, arbitrary surrealism, generic geometry, annotation, and global blur are not substitutes.

## Frontend work

Translate the selected relation into real information architecture and behavior. Paths, nodes, frames, progress, and signal color correspond to content or interaction. Use real DOM text and accessible controls; decorative notation must not impersonate functionality. Test desktop and narrow layouts, keyboard focus, and key interactions.
