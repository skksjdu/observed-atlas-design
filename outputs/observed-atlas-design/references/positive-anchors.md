# Positive visual anchors

These bundled images are production references, not templates. Select them by the relationship the new subject needs, never by subject category. User-supplied references override them.

| [`anchor-multi-representation-motion.png`](../assets/anchor-multi-representation-motion.png) | displacement, temporal echo, simultaneous states, directional extension, or one subject crossing representational media | a recognizable identity anchor, aggressive crop, multiple representations occupying one subject, layered motion, scale jumps, overlap rhythm, archival or material counterpoint, and high-energy integration | automatic anatomy or exposed interiors; generic speed trails; using it when the new subject is also a horse and the model is likely to copy the anchor's hero |
| [`anchor-growth-cycle-geometry.png`](../assets/anchor-growth-cycle-geometry.png) | growth, recurrence, maturation, emergence, or a generative process unfolding across linked states | non-interchangeable stages on one route, botanical and material collage, an area-bearing circle or crop coupled to sparse axes and long arcs, active quiet space, and hand-drawn, printed, and photographic states sharing one plane | copying its hand, seed, roots, tree, green-gold palette, exact circles, or vertical sequence; turning growth into a literal infographic; isolating a terminal cross-section like a badge or over-modeling a branch; selecting it only because the new subject is a plant |

The fragment-continuity, multi-representation-motion, and growth-cycle anchors share a deeper structure that should guide OAS without becoming a fixed layout:

- A clear subject identity may be carried by one continuous image or by several fragments with real informational differences.
- Its states retain a shared anatomy, load-bearing relation, structural axis, or source, so the viewer reads transformation rather than unrelated collage.
- State differences change material, structure, time, preservation, function, or drawing regime rather than merely applying another filter.
- State-bearing fragments also share a visible seam—axis, joint, cut plane, material passage, overlap, continued hatch, or causal boundary—so information reads as transformation rather than pasted collage.
- Subjects, fragments, color fields, paper, and linework occupy one shallow graphic plane. Local hatching may model volume without establishing a realistic environment or cinematic depth.
- Local volume stays consistent across that plane: no isolated branch, member, or inset becomes a smoothly shaded cylinder or photographic sculpture while neighboring material remains drawn and flat.
- European classical handmade language and modern geometric composition can coexist. Geometry must frame, crop, connect, pace, or counterbalance the subject rather than turn the page into a HUD or contemporary technical interface.
- Area-bearing geometry and line-based geometry work as a coupled system: blocks, circles, crops, or stepped fields carry mass and interruption, while sparse axes, arcs, and routes connect distant states. Both families follow the same reading path rather than surviving as isolated tokens.
- Their richness is ordered rather than uniformly loud: subject detail may be intricate, but geometry, texture, fragments, and accents share a hierarchy and leave contiguous fields that are perceptually quiet.

This does not require one continuous subject or a universal multi-state overlay. Distributed and multi-subject solutions remain valid when the theme relation genuinely requires them, but they must share the same classical formal envelope and planar spatial logic unless the user explicitly requests another style or depth-led treatment.

Do not carry over the carousel, horse, exposed anatomy, building, facade samples, classical columns, ruins, birds, exact panel divisions, exact text, factual-looking coordinates, or exact layout. Replace theme-specific content rather than deleting its role: a reference's historical or architectural substrate may become a different culturally situated material for the new subject; a circle, block, axis, line, panel, or route may be re-derived with the same compositional job. A biological subject does not automatically inherit anatomy from the motion anchor, architecture does not automatically inherit section windows from the editorial anchor, and a distributed theme does not automatically require stacked bands. Preserve subject anatomy, structure, topology, function, and major load paths even when the reference contains a visually compelling distortion.

Across the set, transfer these behaviors when useful:

- **Situated content:** recognizable cultural, historical, technical, architectural, or material context gives the hero a world. The default European classical *formal language* does not make European buildings, ruins, columns, religion, or ornament mandatory *subject matter*. Replace context with a theme-specific equivalent and never copy the anchors' concrete motifs.
- **Mixed representation:** the anchors combine drawing, print, photography, scan, flat fields, crops, and material traces instead of asking one seamless realistic scene to carry the whole image. Preserve a deliberate representation regime, not cinematic lighting or generic AI atmosphere.
- **Classical craft by default:** unless the user names another style, preserve Renaissance-study, engraving, etching, architectural-drawing, early-atlas, or European paper-collage logic through variable line pressure, hatching, underdrawing, correction, erasure, incomplete contours, uneven detail, and credible paper or transferred-ink material. Old paper and low chroma alone do not qualify.
- **Planar field:** unless the user explicitly requests realism, spatial installation, or cinematic staging, every candidate uses frontal, side, near-orthographic, or compressed views in a shallow layered field. Material richness must not turn into realistic depth, horizon, sky, depth of field, volumetric light, cast shadow, or sculptural CG space.
- **State-bearing fragments:** when an identity appears in several pieces, each major piece must add unique structural, material, preservation, functional, temporal, or drawing-stage information. Color, texture, opacity, location, sharpness, crop, and broken edges alone are not new states; a removed area must reveal another layer or consequence.
- **Coupled fragment seams:** information and integration are separate requirements. A cross-section, remnant, or alternate view must connect through a credible cut, joint, axis, overlap, material transition, continued hatch, or consequence; a meaningful but floating badge still fails.
- **Consistent local depth:** use selective, broken hatching and open contours for form. Continuous tonal modeling, wrapped highlights, strong occlusion, or cast-like shadow must not make one local branch, limb, or mechanism more sculptural than the rest of the page.
- **Distributed relation:** a theme may be carried across several sites, subjects, or episodes rather than one hero. Use panels, separated fragments, or a traversing route only when sequence, comparison, migration, collective memory, or world-building makes that distribution meaningful.
- **Collective topology:** for a group subject, let contact, orientation, alignment, shared negative space, density, or a common route create the first-read whole before individual figures become separate stories. Encircling a void is one effective possibility, not a universal group formula.
- **Authored variation:** when many figures appear, vary drawing stage, contour confidence, line pressure, hatching, transfer quality, crop, and completion. Do not mistake repeated polished faces, bodies, poses, or garment folds for coherence.
- **Geometric organization:** axes, arcs, blocks, frames, crops, stepped fields, and long lines can establish hierarchy, rhythm, counterweight, scale, or connection even when they are not literal measurements. Use both area-bearing and line-based geometry as coherent families on one route; sparse hairlines over an intact hero, or one disconnected block plus one faint arc, are not enough.
- **Controlled chroma:** the work may be black-and-white or colored. Prefer a muted dominant field with a limited stronger accent unless vivid color is required by the brief.
- **Ordered complexity:** preserve meaningful complexity inside the subject while concentrating high contrast, edge density, repetition, and large-scale movement around the main relation. Geometry, material texture, contextual fragments, and signal color must reinforce or yield to the same reading path rather than becoming parallel focal systems. Quiet space may carry a few low-contrast continuations from the existing route when they supply scale, direction, or tension; do not leave an accidentally dead field, introduce a new geometric family, or fill it with repeated grids, arcs, grain, fragments, or accents. Detail windows and numbered samples are optional devices, not identity requirements.
- **Revision continuity:** when a user asks for flatter space, stronger linework, a more handmade classical draft, or less clutter, change the representation and salience without erasing the accepted geometric route, mixed-material counterpoint, or state sequence. A uniform line illustration is not a successful refinement of a mixed-representation poster.

For expressive work, use the anchors to move beyond literal documentation: retain only enough recognizable subject material to orient the viewer, then derive an abstract carrier from the subject's force, interval, memory, or state change. Preserve that carrier only when its origin and consequence remain legible through a semantic anchor and intentional hierarchy. Do not inherit a dark tonal field as a neutral sign of sophistication. Quiet space is an active compositional interval and does not need to be filled with copy.

Before using an anchor, name the new subject's default depiction and prevent it from becoming the full composition. A familiar aerial, apparatus view, portrait, product shot, or scene may appear as a localized semantic fragment; the anchor should help reorganize the subject across material, scale, time, or representation. Styling the default depiction with the anchor's texture or palette is not relational transfer.

## Use in production

1. Choose one primary anchor. Add one secondary anchor only when it supplies a distinct needed behavior; do not average the whole set. If an anchor contains the same hero subject as the new brief, prefer another anchor or explicitly guard against copying its silhouette, pose, anatomy treatment, and composition. Use the growth-cycle anchor for a genuinely staged or generative relation, not as a default for all botanical subjects. Use the distributed-world anchor as primary only when sequence, comparison, migration, collective memory, world-building, or another genuinely distributed relation requires several sites, subjects, eras, or episodes. Do not select either merely to diversify a second candidate; if only route or density behavior is useful, keep it secondary and preserve the primary anchor's visual-language envelope.
2. Generate the poster's primary visual layer through raster or mixed-media production and pass the chosen local asset path to the image-generation tool as an actual reference. Describe the reference as an editorial system of mixed representations and geometry, not as a request for a photoreal reinterpretation. Unless the user has named another style, explicitly preserve the default European classical drawing and material logic while replacing the anchor's subject context. Unless the brief calls for depth-led work, keep every candidate planar and suppress cinematic concept-art defaults such as a centered symmetrical hero, realistic environment, distant horizon, dramatic moon or cloud backdrop, wet reflective ground, depth of field, volumetric rays, cast shadows, and warm glowing windows. Do not merely paraphrase the asset in the prompt, and do not skip reference-led visual production by drawing the complete poster as SVG.
3. State each input's role: visual-system reference only. Replace subjects, text, proprietary motifs, factual-looking notation, and exact coordinates while retaining selected roles such as cultural counterpoint, geometric hierarchy, long-range connection, or material contrast.
4. Decide whether typography belongs while planning the composition. Generate and accept the text-free visual layer with any intended type footprint and layer relationship already considered, then add exact typography and precise geometry separately in SVG, HTML, CSS, or another deterministic layer. If type adds no value, leave it out.
5. For an open-ended expressive poster, generate and open at least two real candidates with different interpretive propositions or abstraction strategies. Keep their emotional register, temporal and cultural distance, medium and craft character, tonal behavior, and documentary-to-abstract distance coherent unless the user explicitly requests style exploration. A prompt description, imagined alternative, camera-angle variation, or ungenerated plan is not a second candidate.
6. Reject a candidate when removing labels, surface texture, callouts, weather, reflections, and dramatic lighting leaves the subject's default depiction, a polished documentary scene, or a generic concept image; when the thumbnail reads as a three-dimensional scene rather than a shallow work; when old paper and low chroma merely cover smooth CG, realistic photography, or uniformly synthesized detail; when repeated fragments can be swapped or removed without losing information about change; when a missing area reveals no new structure, material, historical layer, or consequence; when a state-bearing fragment still reads as a pasted badge because no cut, joint, axis, overlap, or material passage couples it to the subject; when one local branch, member, or inset becomes more cylindrical or sculptural than the surrounding drawing regime; when subject detail, state fragments, geometry, paper noise, and signal color remain similarly prominent across the page; when nominal quiet space is repeatedly crossed by competing marks, feels accidentally disconnected from the composition, or is repaired with an unrelated new geometric family; when area-bearing and line-based geometry form separate dominant gestures; when it collapses to a lone hero plus generic trails or splashes; when the abstract carrier has no readable origin or consequence; when contextual material has been erased rather than translated; when geometry is absent despite being the selected anchor's organizing mechanism, present only as isolated decoration, or visible only as thin overlays that do not affect crop, mass, overlap, or negative space; when a linework or simplification revision erases the prior state sequence, mixed material, or geometric route; when it reads as the wrong physical material; when a supporting metaphor hides the semantic anchor; when the palette is needlessly vivid or imposes an unsupported mood; or when the candidate is internally coherent but leaves the shared visual-language envelope solely to appear different. Preserve a complex subject when it carries meaning; correct clutter by removing or subordinating competing support layers, not by flattening the subject or its compositional system into simplicity. Do not repair those failures by adding more annotation or a superficial style filter.
7. For a collective or multi-figure subject, reject a page of complete independent vignettes that has no first-read group topology, and reject uniformly polished figures with repeated faces, physiques, theatrical poses, garment folds, or equal edge finish. Correct the large relationship and representation variation rather than forcing an encircling layout or hiding sameness under paper texture.

Judge fidelity by transformation, interpenetration, hierarchy, density, material variation, and compositional energy—not by repeated motifs.
