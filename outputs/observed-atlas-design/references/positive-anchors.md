## Learn change and unity from the growth anchor

The hand, seed, shoots, roots, foliage, tree canopy, and tree-ring material contribute different meanings rather than repeat one plant with filters. They can be read as receiving or giving, potential, manifestation, support, expansion, and accumulated time. This is an interpretation of the image, not a botanical claim that every fragment depicts one specimen or a prescribed scientific sequence.

The forms belong through an upward movement, material handoffs, overlap, scale relations, and a related geometric family. Their unity is propositional as well as visual. Different objects do not need one anatomy; views of the same object still need recognizable identity and credible structure. The depicted hand serves gesture and support while yielding attention to the larger relation. Transfer that role-specific distribution of detail rather than reproducing the hand or its scale.

For a new subject, determine which changes matter and which relationship holds them together. Progression, branching, recurrence, comparison, coexistence, and rupture can all organize forms. Do not copy this anchor's seed-to-tree sequence, diagonal ascent, or circles into every work.

## Adapt the visual system to the subject

- Transfer selected relationships and compositional jobs, then re-derive content. An anchor's historical, environmental, cultural, or material world should receive a theme-specific counterpart when that role matters. A setting can remain drawn, mapped, printed, or collaged on a shallow plane.
- Adapt scale, crop, density, edge, and finish as actively as geometry. A large detailed building, mechanism, or animal in a reference does not authorize a macro-scale skin study. Decide what the new viewer should feel first and which details deserve attention.
- Keep essential anatomy, support, contact, and identity credible through selective depiction. For human or animal subjects, distinguish age or structural information from redundant surface resolution. Dense creases, dark joints, enlarged skin patterns, and strong local volume need a thematic reason; softness must not erase age or meaningful roughness.
- Classical and philosophical character can arise through proportion, poise, intervals, craft, time-bearing material, and relationships between parts and whole. Quiet sacredness can arise through gesture, scale, orientation, and silence. Greek, Central European, or religious objects are not required tokens, and an inherited dark or coarse treatment may contradict a tender brief.
- Mixed representation permits different drawing stages and local completion within one coherent field. Material variation must serve states or roles rather than disguise copies. Local inserts need appropriate seams; heterogeneous forms can belong through contact, alignment, shared space, or a meaningful interval without being forced into one physical body.
- When geometry is the selected organizing language, keep area-bearing mass and sparse long-range lines coupled to the same relation. Subordinate them to the intended hierarchy without reducing them to a disconnected block and a token arc.

## Use in production

1. Frame the proposition, forms, world relations, change and unity, intended first emotion, and shared visual-language envelope before choosing the anchor. Select one primary anchor; add one secondary only for a distinct necessary behavior. Different subjects or mechanisms remain inside the brief's envelope unless style exploration is requested.
2. Pass the chosen asset as an actual image-generation input when generating or assembling the raster or mixed-media primary visual. A prose paraphrase, code-drawn SVG, or request for another upload is not a substitute for an available reference. State each input's role as visual-system reference only.
3. Replace subjects, exact motifs, factual-looking notation, layout, and text. Explicitly adapt the new subject's depiction and detail hierarchy. Maintain shallow space unless a depth-led treatment was requested; suppress seamless cinematic space and accidental local sculptural modeling, including modeling produced by dense hatching.
4. Generate and open at least two real candidates for an open-ended expressive poster. Compare actual actors, forms, interactions, state organization, and spatial relationships. A renamed concept, rotated hero, different texture, or the same front-to-print-to-fade construction is not a second proposition.
5. Review meaning and first emotion alongside hierarchy, state information, appropriate integration, geometry, quiet fields, classical craft, and planar space. Judge the skin, faces, joints, and other sensitive areas at full size. A correct contact or readable metaphor can still fail when it creates an unintended clinical, threatening, or grotesque impression.
6. Correct the earliest failed concept, depiction, or composition. Re-draw and redistribute detail rather than adding labels, texture, or global blur. During a style revision preserve meaningful states, material counterpoints, context, and area/line organization; redundant surface detail may be omitted.
7. Deliver text-free artwork by default, with no automatic copy, indices, pseudo-writing, or typography footprint. Add precise non-text overlays only when needed. If the user explicitly requests visible text, plan only that copy while composing, accept the text-free primary visual, then implement the requested accurate editable type separately. An invitation to decide whether to use text retains the text-free default.

Judge fidelity by the proposition, change and unity, relationships, hierarchy, craft, and emotional effect of the actual image rather than repeated motifs or a statement that the reference was followed.
